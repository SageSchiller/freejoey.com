An ongoing, extremely informal log of things that happened, things we are choosing to call "evidence," and updates on the campaign. Not a court record. Not a deposition. Please stop citing this in your defense.

[09-15-1995 22:14 EST] LOG 001

FIRST CONTACT

Somebody uploaded a section of proprietary code to a garbage file. It sat there. Untouched. Waiting for nobody in particular. Then a bored teenager decided to find out what happens when you poke a system you don't recognize the size of. That's the whole origin story. Not exactly a criminal mastermind moment, and yet here we are.

[09-16-1995 03:02 EST] LOG 002

THE FALLOUT

They came through the door before sunrise. Enough agents for a bedroom that did not require them. They left with the floppies, with the machine he called Lucy, and with the kid, in roughly that order of apparent priority.

Nobody has explained what they expected to find, or why a "worm" that no one in the local scene wrote required all of this. The explanation would have to come from someone willing to give one.

[09-17-1995 11:20 EST] LOG 003

WHAT WAS ACTUALLY ON IT

The garbage file was a fragment. Not a program, not a plan, not a manifesto. A piece of something larger that somebody at Ellingson Mineral Company had left where it could be picked up.

What that larger thing turned out to be: a worm, quietly moving money out of the company a fraction at a time, and a second piece of code written to capsize the company's own tanker fleet. The second one was the distraction. An ecological disaster makes an excellent reason for nobody to audit anything for a while.

None of which a teenager wrote, and all of which he is somehow standing closest to.

[09-19-1995 16:45 EST] LOG 004

THE OTHER SUSPECT

Ellingson's own head of computer security is named Eugene Belford, professionally known as The Plague. He is the person who identified the intrusion, the person who told the Secret Service where to look, and the person whose department the compromised file came out of.

He rollerblades through the office. This is not evidence of anything. We mention it because it is true and because it annoys us.

Nobody has searched his office. Nobody has taken his computer out of his home before sunrise. Nobody has asked him a single question he did not volunteer to answer.

[10-01-1995 14:00 EST] LOG 005

WHERE HE STANDS NOW

In custody. No charges announced. No release date given. The agency that took him has not explained what it believes a teenager contributed to a multimillion dollar embezzlement scheme, and has not been asked to.

Meanwhile the disk he hid is the only reason anyone can prove the scheme existed at all. Sit with that ordering of events for a moment.

[10-04-1995 02:55 EST] LOG 006

THE AGENT

The raid was run by Special Agent Richard Gill of the United States Secret Service. He led it personally. He has given no interviews, filed no public statement, and named no charge.

He is also, by his own reported account, a man who does not especially understand what he seized, which has not slowed him down even slightly.

The record notes that within days of the raid, Agent Gill's credit rating collapsed, a personal advertisement appeared in his name in several papers, and at least one government database briefly listed him as deceased. The campaign was not consulted about any of this and has no information regarding who was.

[10-04-1995 03:10 EST] LOG 007

A CALL TO ACTION

So: hack the man's life. Ruin his credit. Sign him up for things. Put him in the classifieds. Make the next thirty years of his paperwork an ordeal. He took a kid's computer out of a bedroom before sunrise and he can live with the consequences.

This campaign has no capability to do any of that, has never had any, and would not know where to begin. Our entire technical apparatus is one BBS with a single node and a website nobody has updated since the Clinton administration. Consider the above a statement of feeling.

Agent Gill is a fictional character in a 1995 film. Everything described in LOG 006 happens to him on screen, at the hands of people considerably more competent than us. Do not do any of it to anybody real.

[10-06-1995 13:00 EST] LOG 008

PRIOR RECORD, FOR CONTEXT

This is not the first time. In 1988 a boy was prosecuted for crashing systems on a scale the papers put at one thousand five hundred and seven in a single day. He was eleven years old. The resolution was a fine his family paid and a court order barring him from touching a keyboard until his eighteenth birthday.

It works, as an approach. It produces a headline, a number, and a child who cannot use a computer. What it does not produce is the person who wrote the thing.

Seven years later, same agency, same instinct, smaller bedroom.

[03-14-2026 09:41 EST] LOG 009

THIRTY-ONE YEARS LATER

This site went up in 1995 and nobody ever took it down. The campaign continues, in the sense that a campaign with no leadership, no funding, and no legal standing can be said to "continue." Somebody still pays for the domain. Nobody remembers who.

[UNDATED] LOG 000 :: SEALED

THE PART NOBODY FILED

He could have handed it over. At any point, any adult in the building would have taken that disk off him and the whole thing would have stopped being his problem.

He kept it. Not because he understood what was on it. He did not. He kept it because he had worked out that it was the only thing in the room anybody wanted, and that the moment he gave it up he would go back to being the kid nobody let in the room.

That is not heroism and we are not going to dress it up as heroism. It is a kid making the only move available to him. It happened to be the right one.

SECURE TERMINAL

Ellingson's own security department protects its systems with passwords chosen by Ellingson's own employees. Their head of security has gone on record about the four most common ones. He considered this an amusing observation rather than a problem to fix.
