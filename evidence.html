Case file maintained by the campaign. Entries are numbered in the order they were logged. Sources are the local scene, the parts of the record that were shared with us, and people who were in the room. It is not a court document and it carries no weight anywhere.

[09-15-1995 22:14 EST] LOG 001

FIRST CONTACT

A section of proprietary code was sitting in a garbage file on the Ellingson Mineral Company system. The directory was not monitored and the file was not protected.

On the night of 15 September an unlisted login copied part of it out. The subject was looking to see what was there. He did not know what he had taken, and there is nothing to suggest he could have read it if he had.

That is the entire act. Everything else in this file follows from it.

[09-16-1995 03:02 EST] LOG 002

THE FALLOUT

TIME: before sunrise, 16 September. AGENCY: United States Secret Service.

Agents entered the subject's home. His mother was in the hallway. They removed the floppy disks, then the computer, then the subject, in that order.

The computer is a personal machine the subject calls Lucy. It was carried down the stairs while he watched.

DISPOSITION: no charge announced at the scene. None announced since.

[09-17-1995 11:20 EST] LOG 003

WHAT WAS ON THE DISK

CHAIN OF CUSTODY: the file was copied off the Ellingson system and written out to a single 3.5 inch floppy. That disk is the item in question. It was not among the floppies taken from the bedroom and it was not on the computer they carried down the stairs, because the subject had already put it somewhere else.

What is on it is a fragment of something larger. Not a program, not a plan, not a document. On its own it does nothing at all.

The larger thing is two pieces of code. The first is a worm taking money out of Ellingson Mineral Company in amounts small enough to read as rounding. The second is the da Vinci virus, written to capsize the company's own tanker fleet.

The second exists to cover the first. A spill occupies every auditor and every reporter for months.

NOTE FOR THE FILE: neither piece was written by the subject. Both were written inside the company.

[09-19-1995 16:45 EST] LOG 004

THE OTHER SUSPECT

SUBJECT: Eugene Belford. Handle: The Plague. Head of computer security, Ellingson Mineral Company.

He identified the intrusion. He reported it. He directed the agency to the subject. The compromised file came out of his own department.

He has not been searched. His home computer has not been seized before sunrise. He has answered no question he did not first offer to answer.

NOTE FOR THE FILE: he rollerblades through the office. This has no bearing on anything.

[09-20-1995 19:30 EST] LOG 005

THE SECOND ARREST

SUBJECT: Ramon Sanchez. Handle: Phantom Phreak. Also answers to the King of NYNEX, a title nobody in the five boroughs has ever contested.

LOCATION: a payphone, transit station, below street level. Agents were waiting at the top of the stairs. He was mid-call. He did not run and there is no report of him raising his voice.

DISPOSITION: held. No charge filed. No date set. Second minor taken in five days.

NOTE FOR THE FILE: whatever list the agency is working from has more names on it than the file has evidence.

[10-01-1995 14:00 EST] LOG 006

WHERE HE STANDS NOW

STATUS: held. No charge filed. No release date set. No counsel named in any paperwork this campaign has been able to obtain.

The agency has not stated what it believes a teenager contributed to a scheme moving money out of a mineral company. It has not been asked.

NOTE FOR THE FILE: the only physical evidence that the scheme exists at all was recovered from the subject. The subject remains in custody.

[10-04-1995 02:55 EST] LOG 007

THE AGENT

SUBJECT: Richard Gill. Special Agent, United States Secret Service.

He ran the raid personally. He has given no interview, filed no public statement, and named no charge.

By his own reported account he does not understand what he seized. He seized it anyway.

SUBSEQUENT EVENTS, FOR THE RECORD: within days of the raid Agent Gill's credit rating collapsed, a personal advertisement ran in his name in several papers, and at least one federal database briefly listed him as deceased. The campaign has no information about who arranged any of it.

[10-04-1995 03:10 EST] LOG 008

A CALL TO ACTION

FILING NOTE: the entry below was logged at three in the morning by whoever had the keyboard. It is kept in the file because striking it would be dishonest.

Hack the man's life. Ruin his credit. Sign him up for things. Put him in the classifieds. Make the next thirty years of his paperwork an ordeal. He took a kid's computer out of a bedroom before sunrise and he can live with the consequences.

This campaign has no capability to do any of that, has never had any, and would not know where to begin. Its entire technical apparatus is one BBS with a single node and a website nobody has updated since the Clinton administration. Read the above as a statement of feeling.

Agent Gill is a fictional character in a 1995 film. Everything described in LOG 007 happens to him on screen, at the hands of people considerably more competent than us. Do not do any of it to anybody real.

[10-06-1995 13:00 EST] LOG 009

PRIOR RECORD, FOR CONTEXT

PRIOR CASE: 1988. A boy prosecuted for crashing systems on a scale the papers put at one thousand five hundred and seven in a single day. Eleven years old. Resolution: a fine his family paid, and a court order barring him from a keyboard until his eighteenth birthday.

He did it. That case found the person responsible and closed on him.

NOTE FOR THE FILE: seven years later, same agency, smaller bedroom, and the department the file came out of has not been asked a question.

[03-14-2026 09:41 EST] LOG 010

This site went up in 1995 and nobody ever took it down. The campaign has no leadership, no funding, and no legal standing. It has a domain, which somebody still pays for. The records do not show who.

STATUS: open. No charge was ever filed and no finding was ever entered, so there has never been anything to close.

[UNDATED] LOG 000 :: SEALED

THE PART NOBODY FILED

He could have handed it over. At any point, any adult in the building would have taken that disk off him and the whole thing would have stopped being his problem.

He kept it. Not because he understood what was on it. He did not. He kept it because it was the only thing in the room anybody wanted, and because the moment he handed it over he went back to being the one nobody let in the room.

This entry records a motive, not a virtue. It was the only move available to him. It happened to be the right one.
