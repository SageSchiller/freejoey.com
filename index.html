They called him a script kiddie. We call him a scapegoat.

In the fall of 1995, a kid with no handle and nothing to prove it with dialed into a system he had no way of understanding the size of, and came back out holding a garbage file. He did not know what was in it. At that point nobody did.

What was in it: a fragment of a worm, moving money out of the Ellingson Mineral Company a slice at a time, and alongside it a second piece of code written to capsize the company's own tanker fleet. The spill was the cover. An ecological disaster buys a great many months in which nobody audits anything. A teenager did not write that. A teenager could not have written that.

The Secret Service came through his front door before sunrise. They took the floppies. They took the machine he called Lucy, carried out past his mother in the hallway. Then they took him.

They came for The Phantom Phreak next, at a payphone in a transit station, mid-call: the King of NYNEX arrested inside the network he understood better than the people being paid to run it. Two kids in custody inside a week.

In the same week, Eugene Belford went to work as normal. The Plague. Ellingson's own head of computer security, the man who reported the intrusion, whose department the file came out of, and who has still not been asked to sit down and account for any of it.

Here is the part that should have settled everything. Joey kept the disk. He put it somewhere they did not look while they were carrying his monitor down the stairs, and it turned out to be the only hard evidence that any of this was an inside job. The case against the people who actually did it exists because the kid nobody took seriously held on to something he could not read.

None of this is new. In 1988 they prosecuted an eleven-year-old for crashing systems on a scale the papers put at one thousand five hundred and seven in a single day. They fined his family and barred him from a keyboard until his eighteenth birthday. You know him now as Zero Cool. The approach produces a headline, a number, and a child who cannot use a computer. What it has never once produced is the person who wrote the thing.

FREE JOEY is a grassroots and entirely unofficial campaign. You already know the format. You have seen the stickers, the banner ads, the signs at the back of the meeting. The format exists because it works, and because the one they pick up is so reliably the one least equipped to be picked up.

He was not the best hacker in the room. Most days he was not allowed in the room. He is a kid who took a file he could not read, and he is still the only reason anybody can prove what was in it.
