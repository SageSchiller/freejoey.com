They called him a script kiddie. We call him a scapegoat.

In the fall of 1995, a kid with a 300-baud modem and more confidence than clearance downloaded a garbage file he had absolutely no business touching. He didn't write the worm sitting inside it. He didn't design the embezzlement scheme it was hiding. He just poked a system he didn't understand the size of — and every hacker in New York got blamed for what happened next.

History remembers Zero Cool. History remembers Acid Burn, Cereal Killer, Lord Nikon, The Phantom Phreak. History does not spend a lot of time on the guy who actually took the hardest fall for it: Joey.

He wasn't the best hacker in the room. Most days he wasn't even allowed in the room. He social-engineered his way to a modem upgrade, dialed a number he shouldn't have had, and single-handedly got the entire local scene raided by the Secret Service for it. And when it actually mattered — when the Gibson needed saving — he still showed up.

We think that's worth something.

FREE JOEY is a grassroots, entirely unofficial, and frankly unnecessary campaign in the tradition of the real 1990s "Free Kevin" movement that once rallied hacker solidarity behind Kevin Mitnick. The key difference: nobody arrested Joey. Nobody is holding him anywhere. He is, legally and narratively, a free man. We just think he deserves a website anyway.
